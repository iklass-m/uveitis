Uveitis — Full Front-of-Patient Workup

Six steps: History (what to ask, with follow-ups) → Review of systems (branching red-flag screen) → Eye signs (what you see at the slit lamp) → Imaging (OCT, FA, ICGA, FAF signatures) → Investigations (what to order, why, and what each test can and cannot do) → Results (plug the numbers in). The differential ranks live across all of them, and Summary writes it up for your notes.
Grounded in your library — Foster & Vitale Diagnosis & Treatment of Uveitis 2e; Samalia in Uveitis Management: A Clinical Handbook (2023) ch.2; Wills Eye Manual 7e §12.1; Oxford Handbook of Ophthalmology 4e. Decision-support only — your judgement governs.
Quick differential. Type what the patient has, in your own words — age, laterality, course, signs, imaging, results. Negatives count too (“no vitritis”, “no synechiae”). It ranks the differential, shows why, and suggests next tests. Runs on the phone; nothing is sent or saved.
Keyword matching for decision support — check the “Recognised” list, and work through the tabs for a full assessment.
What you see at the slit lamp. Tap the findings present. Each one is a discriminating sign — the panel updates the differential and, where a sign is near-specific (mutton-fat KP, sectoral iris atrophy, mobile hypopyon), tells you what it points to and why. Work anterior → posterior.
Multimodal imaging. Tap what the OCT, FA, ICGA, FAF or B-scan shows — each finding feeds the differential like an eye sign. Below: the imaging signature of each condition, from your BCSC notes. FA = retinal vessels and leakage · ICGA = choroid (wait 20–30 min) · FAF = RPE activity · OCT = macula and outer retina · EDI-OCT / B-scan = choroidal thickness and sclera.
